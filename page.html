WALL STREET

Nasdaq plunges 4.35% in worst November rout since 2008

NEW YORK — Wall Street's sell-off deepened on Wednesday: the Nasdaq tumbled as much as 4.35% intraday and the Dow Jones slid 1,039 points, putting the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 on course for their worst November since 2008. The VIX fear gauge jumped to about 32.

US INFLATION

Core US inflation hits 3.3%, the highest in three years

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE, rose 3.3% in the year to October — above forecasts and the highest since November 2023. Markets now bet the Fed will raise rates by 25 basis points in December.

CHIP STOCKS

Nvidia slides nearly 9% as AI stocks lead the rout

Nvidia fell as much as 8.9% to a low of $141.22 and Micron dropped 10% as chipmakers bore the brunt of the sell-off; the Philadelphia semiconductor index lost nearly 7%.

BITCOIN

Bitcoin dips to $57,209, then claws back $58,000

Bitcoin touched $57,209 before recovering above $58,000, with about $511 million of crypto positions liquidated in 24 hours. The Fear & Greed Index sat at 13 — a 13th straight day of extreme fear.

GOLD

Gold breaks below $3,500 as havens fail to hold

Spot gold slid to $3,469.30 an ounce, a 14-month low and some 37% below January's record, as the rush for safety failed to lift bullion. Silver fell below $40 for the first time since August 2025.

CHINA · TARIFFS

Beijing calls US overcapacity claim a 'false premise'

China's foreign ministry dismissed Washington's Section 301 tariff case as political manipulation built on a 'false premise' of industrial overcapacity, urging talks on an equal footing while naming no countermeasures.

US HOUSING

New-home sales jump 14% — within a huge margin of error

US new-home sales rose to an annual pace of 701,000 in October, beating forecasts — though the Census Bureau put the margin of error at ±22.6 points, and the entire gain came from the South.
